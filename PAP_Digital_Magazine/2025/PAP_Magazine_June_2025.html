ON THE PHILOSOPHICAL REVIVAL OF EVERYDAY MATERIALS

The Manifesto of the Awakening of Things: On the Philosophical Revival of Everyday Materials is a fashion editorial grounded in Martin Heidegger’s notion of the “ready-to-hand.” Titled Handy Things, the series visually explores how everyday discarded materials regain meaning through physical interaction with the human body, reclaiming their existential value beyond their status as mere consumer objects. Materials such as synthetic wigs, shipping box fibers, and soda can pull rings are no longer passive industrial waste. Through street-based bodily engagement, they transform into “handy things”, objects that live, express, and respond. The editorial is structured around five existential experiments, in which each look becomes a moment of dialogue between body and object: a red cotton off-the-shoulder shirt with thick plastic chain, “LOVE” printed stockings that deform with movement, and a plush pendant visibly glued to a crocodile-style bag all illustrate the friction between mass production and intimate use. Throughout the series, the marks of usage are deliberately preserved. The bent creases in a denim sandal zipper, the force-formed angles of a plastic wig, and a sudden pink bow on a leopard bag all serve as tactile records of interaction. Set against a dark, dreamlike backdrop, these so-called “cheap” materials begin to deconstruct aesthetic hierarchies, reinterpreting mass-market artifacts as postmodern talismans. This is more than fashion, it is a phenomenological rebellion. The project challenges the idea that people merely use objects; instead, it presents a world where objects reawaken through the body, reclaiming their agency, their presence, and their right to exist. Each material becomes a body in itself, longing for reconciliation with being.

LUMINESCENCE
